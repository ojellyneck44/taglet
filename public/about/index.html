About Taglet

Taglet is a free tool for getting your own music files in order: the right title, artist and album, a proper cover, and an MP3 that plays everywhere, including Spotify’s Local Files.

Why it exists

A lot of music doesn’t live on streaming services: bootlegs, demos, rips of CDs you own, tracks bought from Bandcamp, songs you made yourself. Once that music is on your phone it usually looks like a mess, with “Track 01” titles, no artist, a blank grey square for a cover, and albums split into pieces. Fixing that has meant installing desktop software, learning what an ID3 tag is, and doing it all on a computer.

Taglet does the same job in a web page, on any device, in a couple of minutes. Drop files in, see what’s missing, fix it, and export.

What it does

  • Reads the tags and cover already inside your files, and fills gaps from the rest of an album.
  • Converts WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG and more to 320 kbps MP3, or keeps an MP3’s original audio and only rewrites the tags.
  • Crops and sets cover art, groups tracks into albums, and checks that everything Spotify needs is present.
  • Exports a zip or single files, shares an album through a private link, and can link two devices directly.
  • Optionally keeps your library on every device with a free account.

Private by design

Conversion and tagging happen inside your browser using ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. Your files are not uploaded unless you choose to share an album or sign in to sync. There are no tracking cookies or third-party trackers, only anonymous usage counts. The details are on the privacy page.

Who makes it

Taglet is made and maintained by Stevie McLane, an independent developer. The code is open on GitHub. Questions, bugs and ideas are welcome through the contact page.

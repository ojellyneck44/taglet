How to convert FLAC to MP3

FLAC is perfect for archiving, but plenty of phones, cars and apps want MP3. Here’s how to convert FLAC to MP3 without losing your tags or cover art, and without uploading anything.

Why convert FLAC to MP3

FLAC is lossless: it stores the original audio exactly, so files are large, often 20 to 40 MB per song. MP3 is lossy but much smaller, and it works on virtually every player. Spotify’s Local Files feature, for example, plays MP3, M4A and MP4 but not FLAC, so FLAC files simply don’t show up.

Steps

  1. Open Taglet and drop your FLAC files in, or tap the upload box and pick them. Taglet reads the title, artist, album and cover already inside each file.
  2. Check the tags. Anything missing is flagged; fill it in once for the whole album.
  3. Choose a quality. 320 kbps is the highest MP3 quality and a safe default. V0 is variable bitrate, usually a bit smaller at near-identical sound.
  4. Export. You get MP3 files with the tags and cover written in, as a zip or one by one.

What you lose and keep

MP3 throws away audio detail that most listeners can’t hear at 320 kbps, which is why the files shrink to roughly a quarter of the size. The conversion can’t be undone, so keep your FLAC originals; Taglet never alters them. Tags, track order and cover art are kept. High sample rates such as 96 kHz are brought down to 44.1 kHz, the standard for MP3.

Is it private?

Yes. The conversion runs inside your browser using ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, so your music never leaves your device. The first time, your browser downloads the converter (about 10 MB) and keeps it, so it works offline after that.

Troubleshooting

  • The converted files have no cover. Add one in Taglet before exporting; any square image works and Taglet crops it for you.
  • Songs from one album appear separately. Make sure the album and album artist tags match across the tracks. Taglet groups songs by these.
  • It’s slow on a big album. Conversion happens on your device, so a phone takes longer than a computer. Keep the tab open until it finishes.

How to fix MP3 tags

Tags are the information inside an audio file: title, artist, album, track number, year and cover art. When they’re missing or wrong, your music is a jumble. Here’s how to fix them.

What are MP3 tags?

MP3 files store their information in a block of data called an ID3 tag. Players read it to decide what to show and how to sort. If the tag says nothing, the player falls back to the file name, and you get “Track 01” with no artist, albums broken into pieces and blank covers.

The most common problems

  • Album split in two. The album name or album artist differs slightly between songs, even by a capital letter or a trailing space. Players treat them as different albums.
  • Songs in the wrong order. Missing or inconsistent track numbers. Players sort alphabetically instead.
  • Artist shows as “Various” or the wrong name. Compilations need the album artist set to “Various Artists” and the compilation flag turned on, while each song keeps its own artist.
  • No cover. The image was never embedded, or it’s a huge file players ignore.
  • Garbled characters. An old tag written in the wrong text encoding. Re-saving with modern UTF-8 tags fixes it.

Fixing them in Taglet

  1. Drop your files into Taglet. Songs are grouped into albums, and anything missing is flagged.
  2. Click an album to edit its title, album artist, year and genre in one place, then click each song for its title, artist and track number.
  3. Set or replace the cover art.
  4. Export. Taglet writes clean tags and, for an MP3 that only needed a tag fix, keeps the original audio untouched, so there’s no quality loss.

Tips

  • Use the same spelling and capitalisation for the album and album artist on every song.
  • Number tracks 1, 2, 3 rather than leaving them blank, and set the disc number for multi-disc albums.
  • Keep a copy of your originals until you’ve checked the result in your player.

How to convert WAV to MP3 (and add tags)

WAV files are huge and have no real place to store a title, artist or cover. Converting to MP3 fixes both: the files get about 90% smaller and can carry proper tags.

Why WAV files look like a mess in your player

WAV is a raw audio format from the 1990s. It can hold very little metadata, so most players just show the file name. Songs you bounced from a recording program, or ripped from a CD, often turn up as “Track 01.wav” with no artist and no cover. A 4-minute song is around 40 MB.

Steps

  1. Open Taglet and add your WAV files.
  2. Taglet guesses the title from the file name. Fix titles and add the artist, album, year and track numbers. Edit once and apply to the whole album.
  3. Add cover art by choosing an image or dropping one in; Taglet crops it to a square.
  4. Export at 320 kbps. Your MP3s carry everything you entered, so they look right in Spotify Local Files, Apple Music, a car stereo or any other player.

Which quality should I choose?

320 kbps is the best MP3 can do and is indistinguishable from the WAV for nearly everyone on everyday speakers or headphones. If you need the smallest files, V0 variable bitrate is a good second choice. Keep your WAV masters if you plan to edit or release them later; converting is a one-way trip.

Trim silence at the ends

Bounces often have a second or two of silence at the start and end. Turn on the trim silence option in Taglet’s export details and it removes them while converting.

Private and free

Everything happens in your browser, so your recordings never leave your device. There’s no sign-up, and no limit on how many files you convert.
